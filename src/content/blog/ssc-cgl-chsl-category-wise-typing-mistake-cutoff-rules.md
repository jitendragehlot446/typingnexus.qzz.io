When appearing for Central Government skill tests (such as **SSC CGL DEST, SSC CHSL Typing Test, and RRB NTPC**), candidate qualification is determined by the **Maximum Permissible Error Percentage (गलतियों की अधिकतम सीमा)**.

The Staff Selection Commission (SSC) and Railway Recruitment Boards (RRB) set different mistake relaxation thresholds for **Unreserved (UR), OBC, EWS, SC, ST, and PwD (Physically Handicapped)** categories.

In this guide, we provide the complete official category-wise error cutoff breakdown, medical exemption rules, and calculation methods for 2026.

## 1. SSC CGL Tier-II DEST Error Cutoff Rules

In SSC CGL, the Data Entry Speed Test (DEST) is conducted for **15 minutes** (2000 Key Depressions / ~400 words):

* **Standard Qualifying Cutoff (Tax Assistant & General Posts)**:
  * **UR**: Up to **5% errors allowed** (Max ~100 wrong keystrokes).
  * **OBC / EWS**: Up to **7% errors allowed** (Max ~140 wrong keystrokes).
  * **SC / ST / PwD**: Up to **10% errors allowed** (Max ~200 wrong keystrokes).
* **High Cutoff Posts (Executive Assistant, Postal Assistant)**: SSC periodically enforces a stricter **5% threshold for UR** to short-list candidates for higher grade-pay vacancies.

## 2. SSC CHSL Typing Skill Test Error Cutoff (LDC / JSA / DEO)

For SSC CHSL Tier-II (10-minute test for 1750 key depressions / 350 words in English or 1500 key depressions in Hindi):

* **For LDC / JSA (Lower Division Clerk / Junior Secretariat Assistant)**:
  * **UR (General)**: Maximum **7% mistakes** allowed.
  * **OBC, EWS, SC, ST, PwBD, ESM**: Maximum **10% mistakes** allowed.
* **For DEO (Data Entry Operator - 8,000 to 15,000 KDPH Grade)**:
  * **UR**: Maximum **5% mistakes** allowed.
  * **All Reserved Categories**: Maximum **7% mistakes** allowed.

## 3. PwD Typing Test Exemption Rules (Medical Exemption)

Candidates with physical disabilities (Orthopedically Handicapped / Visually Impaired) that permanently prevent them from typing are eligible for **Complete Exemption from the Typing Test**:

### Exemption Eligibility Criteria:
1. Candidate must produce a **Medical Certificate** from a designated Medical Board / Chief Medical Officer (CMO) / Civil Surgeon.
2. The certificate must explicitly state that the candidate is permanently unfit for typing duties.
3. Candidate must upload / submit this exemption proforma to the regional SSC/RRB office before or on the designated reporting date.
4. **Merit Calculation**: For exempt candidates, their final merit ranking is computed strictly on their Tier-I / Tier-II written examination scores without typing penalty.

## 4. How Error Percentage is Calculated in Exam Software

$$\text{Error Percentage (\%)} = \left(\frac{\text{Total Full Mistakes} + (\text{Total Half Mistakes} \times 0.5)}{\text{Total Master Words in Passage}}\right) \times 100$$

* **Example**: If a 350-word passage was assigned in SSC CHSL and you made 14 Full Mistakes and 8 Half Mistakes:
  $$\text{Total Errors} = 14 + (8 \times 0.5) = \mathbf{18 \text{ Errors}}$$
  $$\text{Error \%} = \left(\frac{18}{350}\right) \times 100 = \mathbf{5.14\%}$$
* **Result**: Since $5.14\% < 7\%$, a UR candidate is **QUALIFIED**!

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1: What is the maximum mistake percentage allowed for UR category in SSC CHSL typing?**  
**Answer**: In SSC CHSL for LDC/JSA posts, the permissible mistake cutoff for General (UR) category is **7%**.

**Q2: What is the error cutoff for OBC and EWS categories in SSC CGL DEST?**  
**Answer**: For OBC and EWS categories in SSC CGL DEST, the permissible error threshold is typically between **7% and 10%** depending on post preference.

**Q3: Is the typing test marks added to the final merit list in SSC?**  
**Answer**: In SSC CGL and CHSL, the typing test is strictly **qualifying in nature** (Pass/Fail). Its marks are not added to the written score, but passing is mandatory for appointment.

**Q4: Can visually impaired or OH candidates get extra time in typing tests?**  
**Answer**: Yes, PwD candidates opting for a scribe or taking the test with compensatory time are granted **5 extra minutes** (total 15 minutes for a 10-minute test).
